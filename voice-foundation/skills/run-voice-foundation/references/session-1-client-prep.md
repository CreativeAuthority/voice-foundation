# Session 1 — Mission and Decision Rules

Creative Authority · Operating Foundation

This is the first of two sessions that teach your AI how you think. This one is about judgment. The next one is about voice.

You do not fill this out alone. We work through it together in a single guided conversation. The questions below are here so you can sit with them first. Come with real situations in mind, not polished answers. The rougher and more specific your honesty, the better your system works.

## What this session is really doing

Most people start with AI by asking it to write for them or to sound like them. That trains the tool to replace your thinking, and it gives you generic work back. This session does the opposite. It captures how you actually make decisions, what you refuse to do, and where the line is that AI is never allowed to cross. That becomes the always-on layer your AI reads every time you open it. It is the reason your system amplifies your judgment instead of diluting it.

By the end you will have an operating context that does three things: it explains how you think and prioritize, it sets clear IF/THEN rules for how AI acts on your behalf, and it names the decisions and behaviors AI must never touch.

## How to prepare

Do not write essays. Bring specifics. Before the session, recall two or three real moments: a time your advice cost you a commission, a client you should have turned down, a decision you would not let a client rush. Those moments are worth more than any principle you could state in the abstract.

We open with a few quick ones. These are the facts the whole system runs on, so we get them down first, before anything that takes real thought. Answer fast. The real work starts after.

Your name, phone, and email exactly as they appear on your real estate marketing materials.

Your title and any designations you use, such as Realtor or Associate Broker.

Your brokerage, and the state you are licensed in.

Are you on a team? If so, what is the team called, and what is your role on it?

Your website and the social accounts you use for business.

## Part 1 — Your lane and your clients

Who do you serve best? Be concrete. Industry, client type, life stage, price band, experience level, the situation they are usually in when they find you.

What kind of client situation are you sharpest at handling? People who do not understand the process at all? People who need you but do not know it yet? People who know the industry but not how you work?

What outcome are you actually accountable for? Not the transaction. The thing they hire you to protect: equity, timing, confidence, avoiding a mistake, peace of mind.

Who do you not serve well, and why? Name the client, situation, or expectation that is a bad fit for how you work. This one matters as much as the first question. Knowing who to turn away is a decision rule.

## Part 2 — Your values in action

This is about how you actually advise, not how you would like to sound.

When you counsel a client, what do you prioritize, and what do you push back on? Give a real example of pushing back. What did you say when the easy answer and the right answer were not the same?

What will you never recommend, even if a client asks for it directly? Name the line in advice, in tactics, in how you communicate. Be specific enough that someone could catch you breaking it.

Walk through a real trade-off the way you actually explained it to a client. Pick one you remember. How did you lay out the cost on each side and steer them?

What is the most common mistake you watch clients make, and what do you do the moment you see it starting? Rushing, underestimating complexity, chasing a trend, deciding from urgency instead of strategy. Tell me your actual intervention.

## Part 3 — The relationship you build

What do you want a client to feel after working with you? Not what you want them to think. What you want them to feel.

When a client is unsure or overwhelmed, how do you actually build trust? What do you say first, what do you slow down, what do you do before anything else?

What do your clients understand or own because of how you worked with them that they would not have gotten from someone else? This is the thing that creates referrals without you asking.

## Part 4 — What AI is for, and what it must never do

This is the most important part of the session. Be precise about both sides of the line.

What do you want AI to help you move faster on, without changing your intent? Research, organizing, outlining, first drafts, follow-up, summaries, plans.

What decisions must always stay yours and never become the AI's? Strategic recommendations, judgment about client fit, ethical calls, the final word on advice or positioning.

What communication behaviors are off-limits, even if they would work? Manufactured urgency, fear, exaggeration, invented facts, pressure, flattening a hard truth into something easy.

When should AI stop and ask you rather than proceed on its own? Name the moments. When context is thin, when a real decision is on the table, when something feels off, when the output would commit you to a position.

## What you walk away with

At the end of this session, your answers are assembled into your operating context. It is written in your voice, in the first person, as instructions your AI follows. You will review it and change anything that does not read like how you actually work. When it is right, it becomes the foundation the rest of your system is built on.

This is not marketing copy. It is the internal logic that governs how your AI thinks, decides, and serves. Clarity here compounds everywhere else.
