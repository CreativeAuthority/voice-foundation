# Content types

Each entry gives the audience, the shape, and what breaks it. Read only the one you need.

Two rules apply to everything here. The agent's own foundation outranks the shape guidance below: if their Rules say they open with the bottom line, they open with the bottom line regardless of the structure suggested. And the agent's MLS rules and broker policy outrank both, always.

---

## Listing description (MLS)

Audience: buyers scanning twenty listings, and the agents advising them. They are looking for a reason to stop.

Length: check the agent's MLS character limit before drafting and ask if it is not known. Common limits run from roughly 1,000 to 4,000 characters, and a description cut off mid-sentence in the feed looks careless.

Shape: lead with the one true distinctive thing about this house. Not the price, not the bed and bath count, which are already on the screen. Then the rooms and features that actually earn a sentence. Then location in concrete terms. Close on what living there is like, without telling the reader how to feel.

What breaks it: opening with "Welcome home!" or "Don't miss this one." Listing every room as a comma-separated inventory. Adjective stacking ("stunning, gorgeous, immaculate"). ALL CAPS. Exclamation points doing the work a specific detail should do. Anything that could be about a different house.

### Fair housing rules, no exceptions

Federal law protects race, color, national origin, religion, sex including sexual orientation and gender identity, familial status, and disability. Michigan adds age and marital status. Local ordinances may add more. This is a drafting guardrail, not legal advice, and the agent's broker and MLS rules govern.

- Describe the property, never the buyer. No "perfect for a young family," "ideal for empty nesters," "bachelor pad," "great starter home for newlyweds."
- No demographic or character claims about the neighborhood or its people. No "safe," "crime-free," "exclusive," "quiet family neighborhood," "up and coming," "traditional."
- No references to religious institutions as amenities.
- Accessibility is described factually as a feature, never as suitability. "No-step entry from the garage" is fine. "Great for someone in a wheelchair" is not. Avoid "walking distance"; give the actual distance or say "near."
- Schools: state the district factually only if the agent asks for it, and never with a quality claim. "Great schools" is a steering risk and adds nothing a buyer cannot verify.
- Never reference why the seller is selling in terms that reveal a protected status: divorce, death, illness, job loss, financial hardship.

If the agent supplies language that violates any of this, do not quietly rewrite it. Flag it in one line, explain which protected class it touches, and offer the compliant version.

## Listing description (long form, for the agent's own site or social)

Same facts, no MLS character limit, and the agent's own register rather than MLS house style. The fair housing rules above apply identically. This is the version where their voice does the most work, so weight it toward what they noticed rather than what the spec sheet says.

## Social caption

Audience: people who already know the agent, scrolling. The photo does the showing; the caption does the thinking.

Length: two to five short lines for a single image. Longer only when there is a real idea in it.

Shape: open with the specific observation, not the announcement. The property or news is context, the agent's read is the content. One clear next move at the end if there is one, in their words.

What breaks it: hashtag walls, "Swipe to see," "Link in bio" as the only call to action, emoji doing the work of a sentence unless the agent's own patterns use them, and any hook that could be pasted over any other post.

---

## Just listed and just sold

The trap is that these are announcements about the agent dressed as news for the reader. Give the reader something: what this sale says about the street, the segment, what surprised the agent about the outcome.

Never publish anything about a client's situation, price negotiation, or motivation without the client's approval. Never imply an outcome the agent cannot substantiate ("sold over asking in one day" only if true and permitted by the MLS).

---

## Client email (one-to-one)

Audience: one person who knows the agent. Use their one-to-one register, which is warmer than anything public.

Shape: the answer or the finding first, then the support, then what happens next. Short paragraphs. No preamble about being excited to share.

What breaks it: the register slipping into broadcast, hedging on facts that are firm, a closing instruction telling the client how to feel about it, and manufactured enthusiasm.

Sensitive content, pricing conversations, and anything that delivers news the client will not like get extra care: state the finding plainly, cushion the edge, and stop. Do not soften a number.

---

## Newsletter or market commentary

Audience: the agent's sphere and their industry, depending on who they write to.

Shape: their established format if they have one. Otherwise, the concrete hook, the shift in plain terms, what it means for the reader specifically, and one thing they can do about it.

Be intellectually honest about sources. If a statistic favors the point but the source sells the solution, say so. That honesty is the credibility.

---

## Video script or reel

Written for the mouth, not the eye. Use the Spoken Voice section of the foundation, not the Written Voice section. They are two different systems and the spoken one is full of contractions, asides, and dictated rhythm.

Keep sentences short enough to say in one breath. Mark where they would naturally pause. Do not polish the disfluencies out; the foundation captured them on purpose.

---

## Bio or about page

Third person only if the agent's own materials are third person. Otherwise first.

Lead with what they are accountable for, from their mission, not with years in the business. Credentials go late and short. No superlatives the agent cannot substantiate, and no awards language unless they supplied the award.
